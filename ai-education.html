AI Education Research

Engaging Computing Group · Advisor: Dr. Fred Martin

In Dr. Fred Martin's Engaging Computing Group, I design interactive software tools that make the inner workings of AI visible to young learners. I have developed and evaluated these tools with middle school children across after-school and summer camp settings, covering decision trees, search algorithms, image recognition, rule-based AI, and reinforcement learning, and studying how students tell LLM agents apart from human collaborators. Each tool turns an abstract algorithm into something students can watch, manipulate, and challenge. We then study what students come to understand and where misconceptions persist. This work has been published at ACM ITiCSE, ACM SIGCSE TS, IEEE ISEC, ACM SIGCSE Virtual, and EMNLP.

Tools

Decision Trees

FaunaForest

IEEE ISEC 2025

A web-based tool that introduces middle school students to decision trees. Students build and test trees through challenge-based activities and see how each split on a feature leads to a classification.

Search Algorithms

IntoTheRabbitHole

ACM ITiCSE 2025 · Best Paper Nominee

A web application that makes search algorithms observable. Students step through how different strategies explore a space to reach a goal and compare how efficiently each one finds it.

Paper Try the Tool Code

Image Recognition

AI-See

ACM SIGCSE TS 2026

An interactive image recognition module taught to children at an AI summer camp. Activities show how a model turns pixels into features and predictions, so students can reason about why it succeeds or fails.

Rule-Based AI

RuleRunners

ACM SIGCSE Virtual 2026 · To Appear

A two-player collaborative tool that introduces middle school students to rule-based AI systems. Partners write and refine rules together and watch how those rules drive an agent's behavior.

LLM Agents & Human-AI Interaction

DoppelBot

EMNLP 2026 Main Conference · To Appear

A live collaborative setting in which middle school students work alongside hidden LLM agents and try to identify which collaborators are AI. I analyzed and annotated student interactions to characterize the reasoning strategies students use to tell AI from human collaborators.

Reinforcement Learning

TrainYourSnakeAI

ACM SIGCSE TS 2025

A tool that teaches reinforcement learning to middle school students by training an agent to play Snake. Students adjust rewards and watch the agent's behavior change over training.
