About Me

I’m a Computer Science Ph.D. student at Purdue University, where I am exploring research in artificial intelligence and machine learning. My current interests include multimodal learning, interpretability and reliable AI, continual learning, and AI for science.

Before Purdue, I earned my B.S. in Computer Science from the University of Texas at San Antonio, with concentrations in Data Science and Software Engineering and a minor in Mathematics. As an undergraduate researcher, I worked across several areas of AI and machine learning, including data-efficient learning and scientific image segmentation, distributed deep learning for resource-constrained edge devices, AI education, and machine learning for malware detection. My research has resulted in peer-reviewed publications, conference presentations, and undergraduate research awards.

Broadly, I’m interested in understanding how we can build learning systems that are reliable, interpretable, adaptable, and effective in complex real-world settings. During my Ph.D., I hope to investigate these questions while exploring applications where advances in machine learning can support scientific discovery and other high-impact domains.
